He made me *use* a working agentic product as the act of reading his CV. That is the single strongest signal on the entire site. It's not a claim, it's a demonstration. He leads with "design moves revenue" and backs it with P&L ownership and a 9–13% growth figure. Clients eat this up — it's the language of the people who actually sign off on the hire.
3. **The breadth is legitimate, not padding.** 18 years, agency generalist → in-house retail design system + app launch (Dan Murphy's, real shipped work with a "2× audience" outcome) → BCG leadership. That's a coherent arc, not a job-hopper's grab-bag. The Endeavour chapter is the unsung hero of the CV — it's the proof he can ship product at scale, separate from the AI story.
4. **He's current.** "Running a hackathon I built, in London, right now." MCP servers, multi-agent orchestration, autonomous scheduled agents — this is 2026 vocabulary used correctly, not a LinkedIn-influencer salad.

**What did *not* convince me / left me cold:**

1. **"400+ designers worldwide" is doing a lot of work.** Repeated everywhere. It's *reach*, but the phrasing flirts with implying span of control. Sophisticated clients discount inflated numbers, and worse, it makes me trust the *other* numbers slightly less.
2. **Everything in "Built & shipped" is personal/internal tooling.** The LLM Council, Monday the EA agent, the screenshot-cleaner, the terminal email-sender — impressive as a body of self-directed practice, but these are *his own productivity tools.* None of them is a product with users, adoption metrics, or business outcome attached. The one enterprise example (mining-sector field-maintenance tool) is necessarily sanitised to near-invisibility. So the "builds" evidence is real but **small-scale and self-serving** — it proves he *can* code, it does not prove he's shipped agentic AI that *a business depended on.*
3. **Numbers with no denominator.** "9–13% YoY revenue growth" — of what base? Attributed how? "Double-digit conversion lifts" — on what? These are the kind of figures that sound great on a site and evaporate under a reference check. I'd need them firmed up before I put them in a client submission with my name on it.

## 5. Red flags & unanswered questions before I present him

Not dealbreakers — the things I *must* close out before my name goes on a submission:

- **The lead-capture agent.** It's clever, but it gives me a small pause about *judgement.* A candidate who instruments his own job-search funnel is either impressively strategic or slightly too in love with the gimmick — a stuffy client (a bank, a law-adjacent firm) might read it as self-promotional. I'd want to gauge how he'd dial it up or down for a conservative audience.
- **Is he actually available, and why now?** The site is a *campaign.* Someone in a Global Creative Director seat at BCG building a polished personal-brand site with a lead-capture agent is signalling intent to move. I need the real story: pushed, pulled, restless, or comp? Clients always ask.
- **The scale-of-leadership question** (16 direct vs "400+ reach"). I need the true org chart before anyone over-reads it.
- **Revenue attribution.** Firm up the 9–13% before it goes in writing.
- **Geography & mobility.** Sydney-based, "working globally," currently London. Is he relocatable? Visa status for non-AU/AR roles? This kills more placements than skill gaps do.
- **Confidentiality posture.** Admirable that the client work is sanitised — but it means I literally *cannot* show a client his most impressive (enterprise) work. In a reference/portfolio-heavy process that's a real constraint. I need to know what he *can* show under NDA in a room.
- **Where's the design *craft* portfolio?** The site proves taste (itself) and proves code (the agent), but there's no actual *design work* shown — no brand systems, no product screens, no case studies. For a "Head of Design" search, a hiring manager will ask "show me the design." Right now I'd have nothing to hand them but the site itself.

## 7. Verdict + the 5 things I'd tell HIM to change to convert recruiters like me

**Verdict: Shortlist — strong yes for the Creative-AI / Head-of-Creative lane; conditional yes for CDO at right-sized companies.** He's a candidate I'd proactively pitch to clients rather than just file. The site does more to advance his candidacy than 95% of materials I'm sent. It is, however, a *brilliant top-of-funnel* with a *soft middle* — it makes me want the meeting but doesn't fully de-risk the hire. That's fine; that's the meeting's job. But a few fixes would let me move him faster and pitch him higher.

**The 5 things I'd tell him to change:**

1. **Add proof of leadership — testimonials and named growth.** Three short quotes from a Partner/MD, a peer, and someone he promoted. For a *leadership* hire this is the single highest-ROI addition. Right now the weakest-evidenced half of his pitch is the half clients buy.
2. **Add one real design case study with an outcome.** Not tooling — *design.* The Dan Murphy's app or design-system, before/after, with the business result. A Head-of-Design hiring manager will ask "show me the work," and he currently has nothing to hand over but the site itself.
3. **Fix the "400+" ambiguity and firm up the numbers.** State plainly: "16 direct reports; influence across a 400+ designer network." Footnote the 9–13% with a base or attribution. Inflated-sounding figures make me trust the *real* ones less — precision reads as more senior, not less.
4. **Add a "what I'm looking for" line that names the level.** Right now I have to *infer* whether he wants VP, Head of, or CDO, and at what size of company. One crisp sentence ("Looking to lead a design-and-AI function as Head of / VP, ideally Series C+ or a consultancy standing up an AI-labs capability") saves me a call and stops me mispitching him.
5. **Give the lead-capture agent a "for recruiters" mode — and make the gimmick legible as judgement, not vanity.** A one-click "I'm hiring / I'm recruiting" path that asks the *right* qualifying questions (role, level, comp band, timeline) would turn the clever toy into a genuine business tool — and reframe the whole agent from "look what I can build" to "look how I think about funnels and human-in-the-loop."
