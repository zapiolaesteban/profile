# Copywriter / Story Audit — estebangz.com

_Senior brand-copy & narrative read. 2026-06-26. Evaluated against the local `index.html`, the live site, `llms.txt`, `knowledge/esteban.md`, and the CV._

> **Important discrepancy up front.** The local `index.html` and the **live** site are NOT the same copy. Local leads with **"Most design leaders talk about AI. I _build_ it."** The live site (fetched today) still leads with the older, softer **"I lead design as a _business_ — and build the AI that scales it."** This audit treats the **local file as the intended/current direction** and grades that — but the punchier hero is not yet shipped. Ship it. The live version buries the thesis behind a "business of creativity" preamble that reads like every other consultant-creative.

---

## TL;DR verdict — does the story land?

- **The thesis is genuinely strong and genuinely rare** ("builds AND leads — category of one"), and the local hero finally says it in 7 words. The bones of a great story are here.
- **The 3-second test passes in the local version, fails in the live one.** "Most design leaders talk about AI. I build it." lands instantly; "I lead design as a business" does not.
- **The live AI agent is the single best asset and it's under-sold as story.** It's framed as a feature ("Talk to Esteban · live agent") rather than as the _argument_ ("this whole conversation is running on software I wrote"). The proof is sitting right there and the copy doesn't close the loop.
- **Voice is 80% there** — confident, declarative, first-person, mostly hype-free. But it leans on a few crutch phrases ("the medium is the proof," "prototype to production, not demos," "the team is the asset") that are starting to feel like a tic, and a couple of lines protest too much.
- **It reads as a very well-crafted CV, not yet a product.** The sections are resume-shaped (About / Thesis / Builds / Experience / Capabilities / Recognition). To feel _productized_ it needs to behave like a tool you _use_, not a page you _scroll_ — and the agent is the way in.

## The story it currently tells

The narrative arc, local version, is essentially: **"I'm the rare design leader who actually builds the AI"** (hero) → _here's the human behind it_ (About) → _here's my one belief_ (Thesis: design moves revenue) → _here's the receipts_ (Builds — nine things I made) → _here's the career_ (Experience) → _here's my edge_ (Capabilities) → _here's the external validation_ (Recognition) → _let's talk_ (Footer).

That's a clean, logical arc. The problem is it's a **logical** arc, not a **dramatic** one. It's structured like an argument a careful person makes to a skeptic — claim, then mount evidence — which is exactly right for the doubt it's fighting ("is a creative director _really_ technical?"). The "Built & shipped" section is the engine of the whole site and it's the most convincing thing here: nine specific, concrete, non-clienty artifacts. That section alone does more work than the entire CV.

But the through-line **wobbles between two theses** and never fully commits:
1. **"I build agentic AI AND I lead"** (the rare-combination story — the one that's category-of-one).
2. **"Design is a discipline that moves revenue"** (the commercial-creative story — the 18-year career thesis).

Both are true and both are good, but they're _different sales_. Thesis 1 sells an AI-native builder-leader to a CDO/AI-Labs hire. Thesis 2 sells a commercially-minded design exec. The site tries to hold both, and the Operating Principle section (#02) actively pulls focus back to thesis 2 right after the hero spent its energy on thesis 1. For the stated goal — read as **AI architect / builder / AI-native creative** — thesis 1 must clearly win, and thesis 2 should be _subordinated_ as the foundation under it ("I've always treated design as a commercial discipline; now I build the AI that scales it").

## Voice & copy critique

**What works:**
- **Declarative, first-person, no throat-clearing.** "I build the thing, then I lead the team that scales it." is a great line — it's a whole positioning in nine words.
- **Specificity in Builds.** "a weekly 'doctor' agent wakes on a schedule, audits and self-maintains my whole setup… _unattended_" — this is the good stuff. Concrete, verifiable, un-fakeable.
- **The colophon** "Built with **Claude Code** — by me. The site is the portfolio." is the single sharpest piece of copy on the page. It should be _louder_ and higher up, not whispered in the footer meta.
- **Restraint on most hype-words.** Mostly avoids "passionate," "innovative," "synergy," "thought leader."

**What's generic or AI-flavored:**
- **"The medium is the proof."** Used in the hero About AND in the first Build card AND it's in the knowledge base. Once, it's a mic-drop. Three times, it's a slogan you stopped believing. Cut to one.
- **"prototype to production, not demos."** Appears in About, in Capabilities, and in the agent brain. Same problem — it's defensive ("not demos") and repetitive. Say it once, with teeth.
- **"The team is the asset."** Fine, but it's a LinkedIn-ism. The CV's version — "create the conditions where people do the best work of their careers" — is better and more human.
- **"Where I make _the difference_."** (Capabilities title) — soft, generic. Capabilities sections are the most CV-dump part of any site; this title doesn't rescue it.
- **The hero eyebrow** is doing too much: "Sydney · Working globally · Right now: in London, running a Creative-AI hackathon I built end-to-end." The "right now in London" is a fantastic _live_ detail (proof of currency) but it's crammed into a tiny mono eyebrow where it reads as clutter. It deserves to be a real, dynamic line — or its own micro-moment.

**Specific lines that hurt:**
- Live hero: **"where craft, technology and P&L are one job: I turn design into revenue, and I build the agentic AI that scales it."** — three abstractions ("craft, technology and P&L") before any verb. Buries the build.
- **"PSIA-certified ski instructor, Colorado — three seasons. (Discipline shows up everywhere.)"** — the parenthetical tells the reader what to conclude. Trust the detail; cut the editorializing.
- **"A career across _brand, product & scale._"** — "scale" isn't parallel with "brand" and "product"; it's a different category of noun. Reads slightly off.

## The hero moment — rewrite

The job of the hero: in 3 seconds, land **builds AND leads = category of one**, and make the agent feel like the proof, not a widget. Three options, strongest first:

**Option A — lead with the rarity, name the proof (recommended)**
> **Headline:** Most design leaders _talk_ about AI. I build it — and I run a global creative practice at BCG.
> **Subhead:** I personally ship the agentic systems, agents and platforms my teams and clients use. The agent you're about to talk to? I wrote it. Ask it anything.

_(Folds the agent into the hero claim — the CTA becomes part of the argument.)_

**Option B — the two-arena line, tighter**
> **Headline:** I _build_ the AI. And I _lead_ the practice.
> **Subhead:** Global Creative AI Lead at BCG Design Studios — and the person who personally codes the multi-agent systems behind it. Most design leaders do one. I do both. Talk to the agent I built to prove it. →

**Option C — the provocation**
> **Headline:** A creative director who ships code. Not a slogan — a _running system._
> **Subhead:** I run the Global Creative AI practice at BCG and personally build the agents that power it. This site is one of them. Ask it about me.

In all three, the move is the same: **the agent stops being "Talk to Esteban · live agent" (a label) and becomes the closing argument of the headline** ("I wrote the thing you're talking to").

## How the agent fits the story

Right now the agent is **technically integrated but narratively bolted on.** The console is gorgeous (the animated conic border, the EQ bars, voice in/out) and it sits in the hero, which is correct placement. But the surrounding copy treats it as a _convenience_ ("Talk to Esteban," "Grounded on his real history. He'll tell you if he doesn't know.") rather than as the _thesis made tangible_.

The reader needs one beat of dawning realization: **"wait — he didn't just write _about_ building agents, the thing I'm using IS one of them, and he built it by hand."** That realization is the most persuasive moment the site can deliver, and the copy currently never says it out loud.

Fixes to make it feel like proof, not gimmick:
- **Name the authorship at the point of use.** Agent label → instead of "Talk to **Esteban** · live agent," try: **"Ask the agent I built · grounded on my real history, voice or text."** The word _built_ does the work.
- **Reframe the footer line ("He'll tell you if he doesn't know")** — that's a trust signal, keep it, but add the proof: **"Hand-built — serverless function, my own guardrails. You're using my work right now."**
- **Make the suggested chips do narrative work.** Current chips are good ("What has he actually built?", "Is he technical, or does he just manage?"). Add one that closes the loop on the medium: **"How did you build this agent?"** — now using the tool _teaches the thesis_.
- **The "Skip the bio — talk to my AI" CTA (live site) is great** and should survive into the shipped version. It's confident and it bets on the product. Keep it.

## "Productized" recommendations — from portfolio page to product

The site currently _describes_ a builder. A product would _behave_ like one. The gap is experiential, and it's mostly cheap to close:

1. **Make the agent the front door, not a hero ornament.** The live "Skip the bio — talk to my AI" instinct is exactly right. Lead with a near-empty hero: one headline, one line, and a working prompt with the cursor already blinking. The page becomes "an AI that knows Esteban" rather than "a CV with a chatbot." That single reframing is what turns it from page into product.

2. **Close the proof loop in the agent itself.** Add a canned-but-real answer path: when someone asks "how did you build this?", the agent explains its own architecture (serverless function, knowledge base, guardrails) in his voice. Now _using_ the product _is_ the case study. This is the highest-leverage move on the list.

3. **Give Builds cards a consistent "I made this" texture — verb-first, with a status.** Right now some cards link out (World Clock), some don't, some say "Live," some don't. A product feels systematic. Standardize: every card gets a status chip (Live / Internal / Sanitised) and, where possible, a verb-first one-liner of what it _does_, not what category it's _in_.

4. **Name the thing.** Products have names; portfolios have sections. The agent is the marquee feature and it's called "Talk to Esteban." Consider giving the _experience_ a quiet product name (even just "Ask EZ" or treating the whole site as a named build) so the colophon line "the site is the portfolio" has something to point at.

5. **Add one живой/dynamic detail that proves currency.** The "right now: in London, running a hackathon I built" eyebrow is gold — it says _this person is shipping this week._ Productize it: a small "Currently" line that reads like a status, updated occasionally. It signals the site is maintained, alive, and the work is ongoing — the opposite of a static CV.

6. **Resolve the local-vs-live drift before anything else.** A repositioning that only exists in the local file isn't shipped. The single highest-value action is to deploy the sharper hero and About copy that already exist locally. The story is mostly _written_; it's just not _live_.

## 4-bullet summary

- **The story and thesis are strong and rare — but the punchier version lives only in the local file; the live site still leads with the softer "design as a business" hero. Ship the local copy. That's the single biggest win available.**
- **The AI agent is the most persuasive asset on the site and is under-sold as story: reframe it from "Talk to Esteban" (a feature) to "I built the thing you're using" (the closing argument), and let asking it "how did you build this?" become the case study.**
- **Voice is confident and mostly hype-free, but three crutch phrases ("the medium is the proof," "prototype to production, not demos," "the team is the asset") are over-repeated and a couple of lines over-explain — trim to one use each and trust the specifics.**
- **To feel productized, not CV-shaped: make the agent the front door, close the proof loop inside it, standardize the Builds cards, and surface the "currently in London building X" liveness signal — these turn a page you scroll into a tool you use.**
