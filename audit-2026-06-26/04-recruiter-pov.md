## First impression — the 30-second scan

LinkedIn first. "Global Creative Director & Creative-AI Lead, BCG Design Studios." That's a credible spine — BCG is a name a hiring committee respects, "Global Creative Director" reads senior, and the AI angle is exactly what every client brief is asking me for right now. Enough to click through. Good.

Then the website, and this is where he separates from the pile. Most "Head of Design" candidates send me a Notion page, a Behance, or a tidy Squarespace with case-study tiles. His landing line is **"Most design leaders talk about AI. I *build* it,"** and sitting right under it is a working AI agent I can actually interrogate. I typed "Is he technical, or does he just manage?" and got a coherent, grounded, first-person answer back. That is a different category of artifact. The site *is* the portfolio piece — the medium is the message, and for a creative-AI role that's a smart, honest flex.

Did it earn a second look? Yes — comfortably. Inside 30 seconds I went from "another agency creative with an AI buzzword in the headline" to "this one might actually be the real thing." That conversion almost never happens this fast. Most candidate sites lose me; this one re-recruited me.

## Would I shortlist him? For what?

**Yes — but I need to be precise about the level and the lane.**

Strong shortlist for:
- **Head of Creative-AI / Creative-AI Labs lead** at an agency, brand studio, or consultancy. This is his bullseye. The "builds and leads" combination is genuinely scarce here, and it's the role he's clearly aiming the whole site at.
- **VP Creative / Head of Design** at a mid-size company or a regional/divisional mandate where the org wants design leadership that is visibly AI-fluent and hands-on, not just AI-aware.
- **Studio Director / Head of a creative practice** inside a larger org — he's done exactly this at BCG and at Endeavour.

Cautious / conditional for:
- **CDO of a large, design-led product company** (think a public tech company or a major consumer brand with hundreds of designers and a board seat for design). Here I'd slow down. His scale is real but consultancy-flavoured, and I'd want to test whether he's run design as a *product* org versus design as a *services/studio* org. Different muscle.

Not the right fit for:
- A pure **Chief AI Officer / Head of AI Engineering** role. He himself says he's "a creative director who codes, not an engineer who designs." Correct framing — and it's a ceiling I'd respect, not fight.

The Dan Murphy's tenure (7 years, design system → website restyle → app launch) is a real operator track record, not just BCG polish.

Leadership signal is also clean: the "Grow by Growing Others" award and the mentorship-first framing tell me he builds benches, which is what I actually sell to a hiring committee for a senior role — can he scale an org, not just himself.

## Red flags, doubts, and unanswered questions

I'd want these resolved **before** I put him in front of a client:

1. **Almost no visible *client* or *team* creative work.** The portfolio is deliberately all his *personal* builds, because BCG work is confidential. I understand the constraint, and the honesty is fine — but it means I cannot see the design craft of the work his *team* shipped, or any flagship creative outcome at scale. For a Head-of-Design role, a committee will ask "show me the work." His answer is essentially "I can't, it's confidential, here's my personal tooling instead." That's a real gap, not a fatal one, but I need a private portfolio or a reference-backed walk-through to fill it.

2. **The AI builds are impressive but they are personal-productivity scale, not product scale.** A daily EA agent, a council, a hackathon site — these are sophisticated *personal/internal* tools. The one enterprise-shipped example ("field-maintenance scoping tool for a mining-sector client, concept to production handoff") is exactly the proof point a client wants, and it's the *thinnest* described item on the whole site. I'd push hard on that one: what was his actual hands-on contribution vs. the engineering team's, and did it reach real users at scale?

3. **Title inflation risk / consultancy context.** "Global Creative Director" at BCG Design Studios is senior, but BCG's design studio is an *internal services* function supporting case teams — it is not a design-led product company. A naive committee may over-read the title; a sharp one may under-read it ("that's an internal agency, not a P&L-owning design org"). I need to be ready to frame this accurately both ways.

4. **Solo-operator signal.** Much of what's most impressive is *him*, alone, building. The leadership claims are well-evidenced on the CV, but the site's emotional centre of gravity is the lone builder. For a senior leadership hire I want more proof he multiplies a team, not just himself. Slight tension between "I build the thing myself" and "I lead at scale."

5. **Geography & mobility.** Sydney-based, currently in London, "works globally." For many of my searches (US, UK, EU-based clients) relocation/visa is a live question he defers to email. Fine, but it's an early-stage filter I'd need answered.

6. **Confidentiality wall everywhere.** Defensible, but it means a lot of trust-me. I'd lean heavily on references to validate the parts the site can't show.

## The "category of one" claim — proven or asserted?

This is the crux. The site and `llms.txt` explicitly instruct readers (and AI summarisers) to treat him as "category-of-one: builds AND leads."

My verdict: **the "builds" half is genuinely *demonstrated*** — the live agent and the volume of shipped personal tooling are real proof, not assertion. That alone puts him ahead of ~95% of design leaders I see, who at best *use* AI tools.

**The "leads" half is *asserted and CV-supported*, but not *shown* on the site.** I'm told about the team of 16, the P&L, the global practice — I don't experience any of it the way I experience the agent. So the rare *combination* is plausible and well-argued, but only one half is actually proven by the artifact in front of me. He's closer to "category of one" than anyone else in my pipeline, but a rigorous committee would say the leadership half still rests on references and interviews. That's fine — that's what the process is for — but I wouldn't repeat his "category of one" line to a client verbatim. I'd say "rare and credible combination; builder half is verified, leader half checks out on paper and needs reference confirmation."

## What's missing to get me to a hiring committee

- **Scale clarity on the enterprise AI shipment** — the mining-sector tool is the money proof; it needs depth.
- **2–3 named references** I can call: a Partner/MD he reported to, a senior designer he grew, and ideally a client/stakeholder.
- **A crisp answer on the next role + mobility + comp expectation.** Right now "open to Creative-AI leadership OR Head of Design/CDO" is broad — broad reads as undecided. I need him to tell me the *one* role he wants most.
- **Some external validation of the AI work** — a talk, a published piece, a repo, press. Right now it's all self-hosted; a third-party signal would de-risk it fast.

## How he stacks up vs. the typical candidate

The 2026 market is loud with "AI creative directors," but the real talent picture is well documented: AI fluency has become a *baseline* expectation for creative leaders, not a differentiator, and the genuinely scarce profile is someone who pairs human creative judgment with hands-on technical build ([Onward Search](https://www.onwardsearch.com/blog/2026/01/top-ai-jobs/), [Riviera Partners](https://www.rivierapartners.com/insights/ai-hiring-in-2026-what-the-data-reveals-about-talent-pay-and-readiness/)). Most of my Head-of-Design candidates fall into one of two buckets: (a) brilliant creative leaders who *talk* AI but have never shipped a line, or (b) technical AI builders with no creative-org leadership and no boardroom presence. Esteban sits in the thin overlap. Against bucket (a) he wins on proof — he can actually do it. Against bucket (b) he wins on commercial and people leadership — P&L, mentorship, C-suite storytelling. The combination plus the BCG brand puts him in the top decile of profiles I could present for a creative-AI leadership mandate specifically. For a *pure* large-company CDO seat he's mid-pack until the team-craft gap is closed.

## My verdict

Hold him for a large product-company **CDO** until I've validated team-scale craft and the enterprise-AI shipment. He's one of the more interesting profiles I've seen this cycle and the site did real work converting me — that's rare and it counts.

### The 5 things I'd tell HIM to change on the site

1. **Add proof of the *team's* and *clients'* creative output** — even an NDA-gated or reference-backed reel. The leadership half is currently told, not shown; close that gap and the "category of one" claim becomes undeniable.
2. **Make the enterprise-AI shipment the hero, not a footnote.** The mining-sector production tool is your single strongest commercial proof — give it depth (your role, the outcome, the scale) instead of one cautious line.
3. **Pick ONE primary role and say it.** "Open to Creative-AI leadership OR CDO OR Head of Design" reads as undecided to a recruiter. Lead with the one you want; list the others as secondary.
4. **Add third-party validation.** A talk, an article, a public repo, a quote from an MD/Partner. It's all self-hosted right now; one external signal de-risks the whole story.
5. **Disambiguate the BCG context up front.** Make it explicit that you ran a P&L-owning global creative practice (not just an internal print desk) — pre-empt the committee member who under-reads "internal consultancy studio." And state mobility/location clearly so I'm not chasing it by email.
