01  /  About

A creative leader who builds, and who grows builders.

I build the thing, then I lead the team that scales it.

I run the Global Creative AI practice at BCG Design Studios. I founded it, I lead it, and I authored the "Creative Advantage" proposal that positions the studio as the firm's Creative AI centre of excellence. Today that means 33 nodes across the regions and a global lab of 10 creative technologists, taking tools from proof-of-concept to working internal product. I own the ANZ Creative Studio P&L and sit on the global Design Studios leadership team.

I write and ship agentic software, hands-on. Multi-agent systems, MCP servers, autonomous scheduled agents, a native app, custom tooling, prototype through to production. But the building is how I lead, not what I am. I write the first working version so the direction is arguable instead of theoretical, then hand it to people who take it further. The seven-product suite is the clearest case: I shaped it, 24 builders coded it in squads at my orchestration, and it shipped with handover docs so it runs without me.

Then I teach it, so it sticks. I designed the Power Up Weeks and ran them through the regional leads so every region got hands-on in its own timezone, and I write and deliver the deep dives: graph engineering, context engineering, prompt engineering, agent loops, building agents in Claude and in GPT, and how to build your own AI employee.

I lead through mentorship. BCG's "Grow by Growing Others" award recognised it. I build benches, coach senior managers into leaders, and create the conditions where people do the best work of their careers. I previously led 16 managers and their associates, developed that group, took several to promotion and handed them on. Around eight promotions have come out of that. I have also had to manage people out, and that is part of the job too.

Before BCG: almost 7 years at Endeavour Group, where I arrived as a senior designer and left as Creative Lead for Endeavour Drinks, running Dan Murphy's, the group's biggest account and Australia's largest drinks retailer. I built and governed the design system, led the site restyle that roughly doubled the audience, launched the app, created sub-brands, and helped grow a small in-house team into a full agency.

I won't hand work off without a working prototype. Then I hand the craft to people who can take it further than I can.

What I built, and who runs it now.

The programme I run

A global Creative AI practice, and the people who run it

I founded it and I lead it: 33 Creative AI nodes across the regions and a global lab of 10 creative technologists, whose job is to take a tool from proof-of-concept to a working internal product. Under that sits a seven-product self-serve suite that 24 builders coded in squads at my orchestration, shipped with handover docs and briefed to named owners in other regions so it runs without me. I also wrote the playbook, so a node can operate with me out of the room.

Upskilling, globally

Power Up Weeks, run region by region

I designed and orchestrated them, then ran them through the regional leads rather than myself, so the whole practice got hands-on with AI in its own timezone instead of watching one central webinar. Alongside them I write and teach the deep dives: graph engineering (map a real workflow into stages and nodes, lay the orchestration over it, prove the path runs, then automate it as a loop), context engineering, prompt engineering, agent loops, building agents in Claude and in GPT, and a session on how to build your own AI employee.

Delivered · global

A global Creative AI hackathon, conceived and built end to end

Run out of London for the firm's creative community worldwide. I built the whole participant experience: the agenda, timed auto-unlock mission cards, RSVP automation, and a skill-sharing library with an automated review pipeline behind it. Contributors drop in a skill, an automated reviewer assesses it against a published bar, and on approval it publishes to the library.

Skills marketplace

A shared library of 49 skills, with an AI reviewer on the door

The thing that turned my own tooling into something a whole practice contributes to. Drop, automated review, approve, publish. 49 skills in it, and a live tracker carrying 79 cards of work across 15 owners so everyone can see what is being built and by whom.

Live · you're using it

This site and the agent you just spoke to

Hand-built. The agent is grounded on a knowledge base I maintain by hand, answers by voice or text, streams token by token, and runs on a serverless function with my own guardrails. You are not reading a claim that I build agentic AI. You are talking to one.

Native app · daily use

Parla, a dictation app I built from scratch

Native macOS, with an MCP server alongside it, and I use it every day. The transcription engine is the easy part. The dictionary is the product: it learns the names, the jargon and the accented edge cases that generic speech models reliably get wrong.

Publications & editorial

A report engine that typesets from the source document

I rebuilt a 40-page report straight from its original Word file with no InDesign in the loop: real brand fonts embedded, page geometry matched to within a hundredth of a point, exhibits rebuilt as vector from their source geometry. The build fails on typographic defects rather than flagging them, so no word is ever stranded on a last line and no headline breaks mid-phrase. Rebuilding it also surfaced two errors in the published original.

Video, cut in code

An AI video pipeline, from stills to a finished MP4

Headless-browser frames, generated voiceover, motion, a music bed and the final cut, end to end and repeatable. I edit video from the terminal rather than in an editing suite. It produced the 90-second film for the practice launch, and there is a motion engine for branded GIFs and MP4s beside it, plus a read-along tool that plays a script with word-by-word highlighting so it can be judged before anything is shot.

Multi-agent org

A self-organising "Creative AI Studio"

I stood up a small AI organisation: a CEO agent that hired an HR agent, which recruits specialist agents, engineers and a deep-research agent, into a team I direct. I set the goals and the agents collaborate to deliver. It helped power the hackathon.

Multi-agent system

An "LLM Council"

Several independent models each draft a plan, get anonymised and shuffled, and a judge model merges them into one, which makes the answer resistant to any single model's bias. I used it to pressure-test this very site's repositioning.

Executive-assistant agent

"Monday", my daily EA agent

One terminal command: it searches my email and calendar, triages everything into P1/P2/P3, surfaces flags and who's away, and summarises threads. It drafts replies and suggests the right kind of response, then keeps a human in the loop by opening Outlook so I decide and send. I use it daily.

Personal infrastructure

A portable, version-controlled AI operating system

A full Claude Code setup backed by a private repo so it survives any machine: dozens of custom skills and slash-commands, MCP integrations, autonomous scheduled agents including a weekly one that audits and self-maintains the whole setup unattended, and clone-survivable git safety hooks with automated secret-scanning. Plus a terminal email sender with several visual templates, which runs my event invites and RSVP tracking.

Systems others build on

Two deck engines, a design-system layer and a UX skill set

Any source document, a PowerPoint, a PDF, a Word file or a pile of notes, becomes a finished on-brand presentation. Beside it: a token and stylesheet layer in light and dark that is now the standard for the studio's pages, and a nine-skill UX/UI system handed to the design team covering review, patterns, microcopy, dashboards and agent-specific UX.

Client work · by sector only

Coded proof-of-concepts inside the pitch, and we won the work

Design Studios runs as case team services, so I am client-facing through the case team: alongside the MDs, Partners and Principals who own the relationship, alongside BCG X data scientists and engineers, and alongside the client's own technical people. I am embedded and hands-on in that delivery, not briefing it from the studio. I help shape the strategy and sharpen how it is told, then build the working proof that makes it real, inside the proposal rather than after it, and I bring the studio and the consulting team together around it. Multiple proposals have been won that way, across agri-food, energy, convenience retail, beverages and banking, with one opening a pipeline that is still running. The win is never one person's. It is what happens when deep BCG strategy gets combined with design and creative craft in the business, and it shows up as delivery and revenue rather than adjectives.

Resources sector

A scientist team's agentic tool, turned into something usable

A client's own scientific team had built a genuinely working agentic tool with no interface worth using. I did the UI and front-end uplift, then handed it on to a team of around 10 specialists with a proper brief, which they had never been given before, and produced a video demo so the work could travel to people who were never in the room. That is the pattern I am best at: take something technically real but unusable, make it land, and leave a team able to run it.

Prototype to production

Agentic tools taken all the way into production

In mining, a field-maintenance scoping tool from concept through to production handoff, embedded with the engineering team. For an aviation ground-services operator in Asia, a proposal with a full web experience, a working tool POC connected to real data and a video walkthrough, client-approved. For an automotive brand, a pitch microsite you can question out loud instead of reading. Client identities stay confidential, so the craft has to be the point.

Unblocking

Getting it built, and keeping it safe

Most creative leaders stall at enterprise IT. I found and closed a Critical security exposure on our own hosting, a publicly readable git directory, and the fix is now standard practice for the team. I drove a subdomain and single-sign-on path through IT for 30+ gated users, and I work directly with global cyber security on what the firm can actually stand up, so the next person doesn't spend three months finding out.
